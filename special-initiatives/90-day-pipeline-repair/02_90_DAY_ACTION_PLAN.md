# AK9I Detailed 90-Day Action Plan

**Day 1:** Monday, August 17, 2026  
**Day 90:** Saturday, November 14, 2026  
**Recommended operating scenario:** Controlled Recovery + Gated Growth

## 90-day mission

By Day 90, AK9I should be able to answer—without reconstructing the story from memory—five management questions:

1. **Cash:** What cash is available, committed, at risk, and expected over the next 13 weeks?
2. **People/operations:** Who owns each critical outcome, what are they expected to deliver, and are they delivering it?
3. **Customers/students:** Where is every meaningful active prospect/student/graduate in the journey, what happens next, and who owns it?
4. **Outcomes:** What happened after training, what is verified, what is still unknown, and what follow-up remains?
5. **Growth:** Which channels/partners/referrals actually create qualified demand and successful starts/outcomes, and which should be scaled?

The first 90 days are not intended to finish every strategic idea. They are intended to establish a stable, measurable operating system that makes the next phase obvious.

# Phase 0 — Day 1–3: Establish command and protect the enterprise

## Objective

Create enough operating control to prevent new leakage or confusion while discovery begins.

## Actions

### Executive authority and communication

- CEO and Transformation Lead align privately on the 90-day narrative, authority boundaries, decision rights, escalation path, and non-negotiables.
- Issue a concise all-hands operating message: AK9I is stabilizing, protecting students/payroll, clarifying accountability, and building a measurable operating system.
- Publish a temporary decision-rights matrix for spending, marketing publication, contracts, student-impacting decisions, staffing changes, system access, and external commitments.
- Establish a **no side-channel commitments** rule: material commitments must be visible to the accountable owner and recorded in the appropriate system.

### Cash and spending containment

- Build the first 13-week cash forecast using actual bank, receivable, payable, payroll, debt, tax, tuition, canine sales, contract, housing, and known vendor obligations.
- Create a daily cash-position view for the first two weeks.
- Require pre-approval for discretionary purchases, event spending, marketing production, repairs not already under an approved maintenance plan, travel, and reimbursements.
- Separate true emergencies from convenience purchases.
- Stop reimbursement of undocumented or retroactively authorized discretionary purchases pending review.
- Inventory subscriptions, recurring charges, cards, vendor auto-payments, fuel/vehicle spending, event expenses, and other recurring cash drains.

### Access and continuity

- Inventory critical accounts: banking/payment, accounting, Salesforce/CRM, website/domain/DNS, email/admin, social channels, advertising, Google/analytics, SAM.gov, learning systems, HR/payroll, cloud/file storage, telecom, and vendor portals.
- Ensure at least two authorized continuity administrators for each critical organizational account where the platform supports it.
- Remove single-person operational dependency without indiscriminately removing legitimate working access.
- Begin a role-based credential/access register stored in an appropriate secure system—not GitHub.

### Student and delivery protection

- Produce an active-student roster from the authoritative school system.
- For each active student, identify program, expected completion, attendance status, known risk, instructor, housing/transport issue if relevant, and immediate next action.
- Freeze any impulsive mass instructor change until instructional coverage, qualifications, student impact, and transition risk are assessed.
- Identify students at immediate withdrawal/completion risk and assign outreach/action owners.

### Evidence preservation / risk response

- Preserve relevant financial, asset, purchasing, contract, access, and HR records.
- Do not place accusations or investigative detail in this public repository.
- Route credible concerns about fraud, embezzlement, asset diversion, wage/payroll, contractual, or regulatory issues to qualified accounting/legal/HR professionals using evidence, not rumor.

## Day-3 outputs

- temporary decision-rights matrix;
- first 13-week cash forecast;
- spend-control instruction;
- critical-account inventory;
- active-student risk list;
- Day 1–14 meeting/evidence calendar;
- executive issues list ranked: **protect now / investigate / decide / defer**.

# Phase 1 — Day 4–14: Stop the bleeding and establish baseline truth

## Objective

Replace anecdote with a usable operating baseline while controlling the highest-risk cash, delivery, access, and culture failure points.

## Workstream 1 — Finance, procurement, and cash conversion

- Reconcile current bank balances and known obligations.
- Establish accounts-receivable aging by tuition, canine sales, services, contracts, and other revenue streams.
- Assign collection owner and next action to material receivables.
- Review near-term contract opportunities by due date, win realism, delivery capacity, cash timing, margin, and strategic value.
- Implement purchase-request / approval / proof-of-receipt workflow.
- Require competitive quotes or documented reasonableness review for material non-routine purchases; define dollar thresholds after the first spend analysis.
- Track fuel, travel, event, printing, repair, and reimbursement spending separately so abnormal patterns are visible.
- Review recurring vendors/subscriptions and cancel or right-size nonessential services.
- Establish company-asset inventory priority list for portable/high-value assets and equipment.

## Workstream 2 — People, culture, and accountability

- Interview each department: Kennels, Training, Grounds/Maintenance, Office/Admissions/Student Services, Sales/BD/Marketing, Finance/Administration.
- For every role, capture: mission, 5–7 recurring responsibilities, current workload, decisions owned, systems used, dependencies, failure points, and 3–5 measurable KPIs.
- Establish time/PTO/attendance expectations and one authoritative timekeeping process.
- Separate performance concerns from personality conflict.
- Start performance/capability assessment using evidence: reliability, delivery quality, productivity, role fit, teamwork, compliance, adaptability, and institutional knowledge.
- Identify single points of failure and succession/backup needs.
- Establish professional communication standards and confidentiality expectations, particularly around students.
- Create a management expectation that concerns are escalated through facts and defined channels rather than rumor networks.

## Workstream 3 — Pipeline/system baseline

- Complete SHR-001/BASE-001 evidence discovery against actual systems.
- Inventory Salesforce objects/fields, lead sources, ownership, activity history, duplicate behavior, open opportunities, stale records, and current access.
- Map website forms, enrollment agreement, tours, calls, email, events, partner referrals, social, VA/SkillBridge, and trade-show leads to their actual destination systems.
- Identify which system is authoritative for prospect, applicant, enrollment, attendance, completion, outcomes, alumni, employer, and financial states.
- Do **not** configure a new lifecycle until actual behavior is understood sufficiently to avoid destroying useful historical data.

## Workstream 4 — Admissions and public truth

- Validate canonical current course name, duration, tuition, fees, schedule/start cadence, admissions requirements, approval/funding eligibility, housing cost, and financing information.
- Correct or queue correction for conflicting public information.
- Review the enrollment path for unnecessary friction and person-dependent handoffs.
- Establish shared inbox/queue or backup ownership so applicants are not dependent on one individual address or one person being present.
- Define first-response and follow-up ownership; actual SLA target is approved after baseline volume/capacity is known.

## Workstream 5 — Student success and withdrawal analysis

- Reconstruct the prior 6–12 months of starts, attendance, withdrawals, completions, and stated withdrawal reasons using authoritative records.
- Distinguish voluntary withdrawal, academic/performance issue, attendance, financial issue, housing/transport, instructor/service issue, personal emergency, program mismatch, and unknown.
- Identify current leading indicators of withdrawal.
- Start a weekly student-risk review with named owner/next action.
- Observe instruction and student experience before making broad instructor decisions.

## Workstream 6 — Outcomes, alumni, and employer baseline

- Identify accountable owner and current SOP/source for placement/outcome tracking.
- Reconcile completion → graduate → placement-assistance handoff.
- Inventory employer/company relationships without equating contacts to active hiring partners.
- Determine how interviews, offers, starts, self-employment, continuing employment, and verification are currently recorded.
- Inventory alumni communication assets and permission boundaries.

## Day-14 decision review

Leadership reviews evidence and classifies each major issue:

- **RED — immediate corrective action**: threatens payroll, students, accreditation, legal/compliance, cash, security, or continuity.
- **YELLOW — repair during Days 15–45**: meaningful process/capability gap with manageable near-term risk.
- **GREEN — optimize later**: not a current bottleneck.
- **BLUE — strategic bet**: potentially high-value future initiative, intentionally outside core stabilization.

## Day-14 outputs

- verified baseline inventory of systems and sources;
- 13-week cash forecast v2 and receivable action list;
- procurement/spend process v1;
- account continuity register completed for critical systems;
- current-student risk and instructor-capacity view;
- course/public-content discrepancy register;
- employee role/KPI draft cards;
- pipeline bottleneck assessment;
- initial outcomes/employer/alumni baseline;
- prioritized 30/60/90 backlog.

# Phase 2 — Day 15–30: Install the operating spine

## Objective

Turn the baseline into repeatable management routines and minimum viable controls.

## 1. Management operating system

Install:

- daily 15-minute leadership stabilization huddle during the turnaround;
- weekly 60–90 minute operating review;
- weekly cash/AR/AP review;
- weekly student-success review;
- weekly pipeline/admissions review;
- biweekly people/capability review;
- monthly strategic-bets review kept separate from the operating review.

Every meeting uses the same discipline: **metric → variance → root cause → owner → next action → due date**.

## 2. Role/KPI accountability

- Finalize role scorecards for all key positions.
- Each employee receives a small number of outcome KPIs plus required controls/standards.
- Eliminate “busy” as a performance measure.
- Identify work that should stop, be automated, be reassigned, or require additional capability.
- Create coverage plans for critical roles.
- Begin documented coaching/performance plans where justified by evidence and HR/legal requirements.

## 3. Salesforce / shared pipeline MVP

Working with Lane 01 and current system truth, implement or validate the minimum operating view:

- canonical identity/reference;
- lifecycle stage;
- original source and referral/partner source where supportable;
- record owner;
- next action;
- next-action due date;
- last meaningful contact;
- program/course interest;
- funding/route indicator where relevant;
- application/enrollment status;
- confirmed start;
- reason closed/lost/withdrawn where applicable.

Do not force post-enrollment regulated/student-detail records into Salesforce if another authoritative education system should own them; create governed references/handoffs instead.

## 4. Admissions workflow

Design and activate a standard inquiry-to-start sequence:

`Inquiry → qualification → program/funding fit → application → document completion → interview → enrollment-ready → enrolled → confirmed start → started`

For each stage define:

- entrance condition;
- required evidence;
- owner;
- service expectation;
- next action;
- exit condition;
- loss/hold reason.

Create daily admissions work queue and stale-record report.

## 5. Student-success operating model

- Define the completion authority and actual student-status states.
- Establish attendance-risk and withdrawal-risk triggers from evidence.
- Assign case owner and intervention action.
- Create standard first-week, midpoint, and pre-completion student touchpoints where appropriate.
- Establish structured student feedback/service-recovery channel.
- Use instructor observations/evaluations as management evidence, not as punitive theater.

## 6. Outcome/placement minimum viable process

Create an evidence-backed outcome path aligned with ACCET requirements:

`Program completion → eligible graduate → outcome goal → assistance provided → employer/self-employment activity → interview/offer/start as applicable → verification → follow-up/closed state → alumni handoff`

- Define acceptable verification evidence with the responsible compliance owner.
- Create a 90-day rolling outcome management view.
- Do not publish placement-rate claims until numerator, denominator, cohort, time window, exclusions, and evidence are defensible.

## 7. Marketing and brand QA

Install a release workflow for material public content:

`request → draft → fact check → compliance/claim check where relevant → brand/quality check → authorized approval → publication → archive/version`

Immediate focus:

- course catalog consistency;
- pricing/duration/approval status;
- VA/SkillBridge claims;
- graduation/career claims;
- outdated pages;
- duplicate social/profile ownership;
- marketing material quantities/cost discipline;
- secure multi-admin ownership.

## Day-30 exit criteria

AK9I should have a functioning operating cadence, visible cash plan, active-student risk management, role/KPI ownership, an agreed pipeline MVP, controlled admissions workflow, outcome-verification process design, and public-content governance.

# Phase 3 — Day 31–45: Repair the back half of the pipeline

## Objective

Make completion, outcomes, alumni, employer support, and advocacy an operating engine rather than an afterthought.

## Graduate/outcome recovery sprint

- Build de-identified cohort lists from recent graduates needing follow-up.
- Segment: employed/verified, self-employed/verification needed, continuing employment, seeking, not seeking, unreachable, unknown.
- Conduct structured follow-up through authorized systems/staff.
- Capture assistance actions and verification state.
- Use missing data as a management backlog, not as inferred success or failure.

## Employer network activation

- Build authoritative employer/recruiter roster.
- Classify relationships: signal/contact, active relationship, active hiring relationship, measurable contribution.
- Assign relationship owner and next action.
- Ask employers for current hiring needs, candidate requirements, location, pay range where appropriate, and willingness to engage with graduates.
- Create recruiter-to-graduate matching cadence without publishing personal details in GitHub.
- Capture employer feedback on graduate readiness for curriculum/service improvement.

## Alumni reactivation

- Inventory available alumni contact permissions/channels.
- Launch a controlled re-engagement effort focused first on value: career updates, continuing education, employer introductions, events, resources.
- Request updated outcome status using a structured and permission-aware process.
- Create alumni segments by graduation year/program/outcome/interest where legally and operationally appropriate.

## Advocacy/service recovery

- Define advocacy eligibility with Lane 03/Lane 02: successful experience/outcome and appropriate consent, not mere completion.
- Establish feedback-before-ask principle.
- Route negative/neutral feedback into service recovery.
- Only then request reviews/testimonials/referrals from eligible willing advocates.
- Preserve consent/provenance for marketing reuse.

## Day-45 outputs

- outcome backlog reduced and categorized;
- employer roster with ownership/activity;
- alumni reactivation process active;
- feedback/review/referral operating workflow;
- employer/curriculum feedback loop designed;
- first closed-loop examples traceable from graduate outcome → advocacy/referral or employer relationship.

# Phase 4 — Day 46–60: Repair learning and admissions conversion

## Objective

Use downstream truth to improve the student experience and intake quality.

## Curriculum/delivery feedback

- Compare employer feedback, graduate feedback, withdrawal reasons, instructor observations, and course objectives.
- Identify top 3–5 learning/delivery gaps.
- Prioritize low-cost/high-impact corrections first.
- Define instructor professional-development needs.
- Identify curriculum changes requiring formal approval before implementation.

## Instructor/workforce decisions

- Use 30–45 days of evidence to classify critical staff:
  - retain/develop;
  - retain/re-scope;
  - formal performance improvement;
  - succession/recruitment needed;
  - executive/HR decision required.
- Build replacement bench before high-risk separations where continuity matters.
- Tie future incentive/profit-share concepts to a separate approved compensation design; do not promise unapproved awards as a substitute for management.

## Admissions qualification and nurture

- Analyze which lead attributes correlate with start/completion/withdrawal once sufficient data exists.
- Improve pre-enrollment expectation setting around hands-on work, attendance, housing, transportation, physical demands, dog assignment, costs, and career realities.
- Establish nurture sequences for not-yet-ready prospects.
- Create lost-prospect reason codes and reactivation timing.
- Ensure funding route/VA/SkillBridge handling has trained backup coverage.

## Day-60 gate review

Evaluate Gates 1–6 from `01_SCENARIO_ANALYSIS.md`.

If major gates fail, continue repair and do not materially increase top-of-funnel spend.

If gates substantially pass, authorize controlled growth tests for Days 61–90.

# Phase 5 — Day 61–75: Controlled demand and revenue acceleration

## Objective

Scale only what the repaired pipeline can receive and measure.

## Warm-market first

Prioritize:

1. open/stale qualified Salesforce leads;
2. prior applicants/non-starts with legitimate reactivation potential;
3. alumni referrals;
4. employer/partner referrals;
5. veteran/military channels after status/claim verification;
6. prior trade-show leads with lawful/appropriate outreach basis;
7. organic search/social/content;
8. paid acquisition only with attribution and capacity controls.

## Campaign design

Every campaign requires:

- target audience;
- offer/program truth;
- source/campaign identifier;
- landing/response path;
- owner;
- follow-up process;
- qualification definition;
- downstream capacity check;
- success measure tied to qualified pipeline/start/outcome—not merely impressions or clicks.

## Partnerships

- Reactivate 5–10 highest-value employer/channel relationships based on evidence and fit, not logo count.
- Verify current GI Bill/SAA and SkillBridge status and exact program applicability before promotion.
- Build referral/partner handoff into the same pipeline rather than a parallel spreadsheet.

## Revenue-preservation opportunities

- Continue disciplined canine sales/service-contract activity where margin/cash timing/delivery capacity are known.
- Use government opportunity review criteria rather than personality/history to determine bid/no-bid decisions.
- Maintain separation between AK9I opportunities and external/affiliate interests through documented conflict/approval rules.

# Phase 6 — Day 76–90: Prove repeatability and set the next 180 days

## Objective

Demonstrate that the operating system works across the journey and decide which strategic bets deserve investment.

## Closed-loop proof

Select representative de-identified journeys and verify:

- source → inquiry → qualification → application → enrollment → start;
- start → active student → completion;
- completion → outcome assistance → verified result;
- outcome → alumni/advocacy/referral where eligible;
- partner/employer activity → measurable contribution where present.

Capture breaks as next-wave defects rather than hiding them.

## Management proof

Confirm that leadership can answer weekly:

- cash/runway/payroll risk;
- revenue due/collected;
- active students at risk;
- starts/completions/withdrawals;
- pipeline by stage/owner/next action;
- outcome-verification backlog;
- employer/partner activity;
- advocacy/referrals;
- spend exceptions;
- role/KPI exceptions;
- top three enterprise bottlenecks.

## Strategic-bet decisions

For LMS, AI kennel assistant, Working Line K9, CLDA/TSA concept, events/media, real estate/solar, or other large ideas, require a written business case with:

- problem/customer;
- regulatory/approval path;
- market evidence;
- unit economics;
- minimum team/capability;
- cash requirement;
- opportunity cost;
- pilot scope;
- measurable success/failure criteria;
- stop condition.

Advance only bets that do not destabilize the repaired core.

## Day-90 outputs

- 90-day results review against baseline;
- verified pipeline health scorecard;
- updated canonical bottleneck;
- remaining risk register;
- staffing/capability plan;
- cash and operating forecast for next 13 weeks;
- 180-day growth/optimization roadmap;
- strategic-bet decisions: `advance / pilot / hold / reject / needs evidence`;
- repository tracker/current-state updates for work actually completed and evidenced.

# Non-negotiable turnaround rules

1. **Protect current students before increasing volume.**
2. **Do not spend money merely because it was historically spent.**
3. **No material purchase without visibility and appropriate authorization.**
4. **No critical company system should depend on one person's credentials.**
5. **No employee is judged solely by rumor, personality, or loyalty narratives; use role expectations and evidence.**
6. **No public course/outcome/approval claim should be published without canonical fact checking.**
7. **No placement/outcome is counted without the required evidence.**
8. **No marketing channel is “successful” merely because it generates attention or raw leads.**
9. **No strategic idea outranks payroll, student success, compliance, or the current bottleneck.**
10. **Every priority has an owner, metric/evidence, next action, and due date.**
