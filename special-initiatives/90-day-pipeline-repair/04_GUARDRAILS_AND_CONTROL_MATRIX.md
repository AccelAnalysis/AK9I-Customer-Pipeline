# Guardrails & Control Matrix — AK9I 90-Day Turnaround

## Purpose

The purpose of these controls is to **keep money, authority, evidence, customer trust, and institutional knowledge from walking out the door** while the pipeline is repaired.

These are proposed turnaround controls. Executive adoption, HR/legal review, financial thresholds, and system-specific implementation remain human decisions.

A control should be:

- clear enough that employees know what is expected;
- light enough that it does not make normal work impossible;
- evidence-producing;
- proportionate to risk;
- consistently applied;
- reviewable and removable once a better permanent control exists.

# Proposed purchasing workflow

The exact dollar thresholds should be set after Days 1–7 spending analysis; do **not** assume arbitrary thresholds are already approved.

Minimum flow:

`Need identified → request → business purpose → available budget/cash check → quote/reasonableness review → authorized approval → purchase → receipt/asset/service confirmation → coding/payment → exception review if needed`

## Recommended tier design

- **Routine/approved:** recurring budgeted items within defined limits; no repeated executive approval once catalog/budget is established.
- **Manager approval:** non-routine but ordinary operating spend under an adopted threshold.
- **Executive/finance approval:** material spend, travel/events, new vendor, capital item, unusual reimbursement, or spend creating recurring commitment.
- **Emergency exception:** necessary to protect safety, students, animals, property, or critical service; documented immediately afterward.

The goal is not to make the CEO personally approve every bag of supplies forever. The Day 1 emergency control can be centralized; the permanent design should delegate within clear limits and budgets.

# Marketing/publication QA workflow

## Applies to

- website pages;
- ads;
- brochures/handouts;
- event materials;
- email campaigns;
- social posts making material program/outcome claims;
- course sheets;
- financial/tuition examples;
- veteran/SkillBridge/GI Bill statements;
- testimonials/case studies;
- placement/employment claims.

## Workflow

1. **Requester** states audience, objective, offer, channel, due date.
2. **Content owner** creates draft from the canonical fact sheet.
3. **Fact owner** verifies program/price/duration/eligibility facts.
4. **Compliance/claim reviewer** reviews regulated or outcome-sensitive claims where applicable.
5. **Brand/quality reviewer** checks clarity, professionalism, spelling, visuals, URLs, QR codes, and call to action.
6. **Authorized publisher/approver** releases.
7. Final artifact/version/date/channel are retained in the approved content repository.
8. Performance is evaluated against qualified pipeline contribution where measurable.

# Contract and opportunity guardrails

For every material opportunity, record:

- customer/agency/organization;
- solicitation/opportunity source;
- due date;
- scope;
- required certifications/approvals;
- delivery location/timing;
- responsible owner;
- partner/subcontractor involvement;
- conflict-of-interest check;
- expected direct cost;
- expected cash collection timing;
- capacity impact on current students/contracts;
- bid/no-bid decision and rationale;
- submission/award/result.

No person should be able to make AK9I silently abandon or pursue a material opportunity solely because of an informal relationship. Conversely, AK9I should not bid merely because it can; delivery and economics matter.

# Digital continuity minimum

By Day 14, identify all critical accounts and classify:

- business owner;
- primary administrator;
- backup administrator;
- organization-controlled recovery method;
- MFA status;
- billing owner;
- data export/backup need;
- last access review;
- offboarding procedure.

Passwords, MFA secrets, API keys, banking credentials, or private recovery data **must not be stored in GitHub**.

# People-management guardrails

The turnaround must avoid replacing weak management with fear-based management.

1. Employees should know their role, metrics, authority, and escalation path.
2. Accountability is based on evidence and behavior, not factions or rumor.
3. Serious misconduct is handled promptly and confidentially through proper channels.
4. Operational disagreements are not automatically disloyalty.
5. Confidential student/employee/company information is not shared for political leverage.
6. Managers cannot create unauthorized work priorities that conflict with the operating plan without surfacing the tradeoff.
7. Leadership can disagree behind closed doors; once a decision is adopted, ownership must be clear.
8. No unapproved incentive/profit-share promise should be used to obtain compliance or performance.

# Exception log

Every guardrail may need an exception. Exceptions should contain:

- control being bypassed;
- reason;
- risk created;
- approver;
- compensating control;
- expiration/date;
- follow-up owner.

Repeated exceptions indicate the process is wrong, the role is unclear, the threshold is wrong, or the control is being ignored. They should be reviewed weekly during the turnaround.
