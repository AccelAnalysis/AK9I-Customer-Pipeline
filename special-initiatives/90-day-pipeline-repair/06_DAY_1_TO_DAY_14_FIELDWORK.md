# Day 1–14 Fieldwork Plan — AK9I Turnaround

**Fieldwork start:** Monday, August 17, 2026  
**Purpose:** Establish fact pattern, protect current operations, and produce the Day-14 recovery baseline without turning the first two weeks into an abstract consulting exercise.

## Operating posture

The first two weeks should combine **observation + evidence + immediate low-regret controls**.

Do not spend the first week writing a future-state process while current students, cash, people, systems, and records remain poorly understood. Conversely, do not wait two weeks to correct an obvious high-risk issue such as sole account access, uncontrolled spend, a missed student handoff, or materially inconsistent public information.

- `GREEN — operating adequately`;

# Day 2 — Tuesday, August 18: Department listening and workflow observation

1. What is your department's mission?
2. What must happen every day/week for AK9I to function?
3. What are the 5–7 most important recurring tasks?
4. What work is falling through the cracks?
5. What are you waiting on from another person/department?
6. What information do you receive too late or not at all?
7. What decisions can you make today without approval?
8. What do you believe requires approval?
9. What systems, spreadsheets, inboxes, texts, or paper records do you use?
10. What does management currently measure about your work?

17. What should AK9I stop doing?
18. What should AK9I start doing?
19. If you were CEO for 30 days, what is the first problem you would fix?

# Interview discipline

The first two weeks will surface contradictory accounts and emotionally charged history. Use this rule:

**A person's statement is evidence that the person said/believes something; it is not automatically evidence that the underlying event occurred exactly as described.**

When a statement matters to cash, student welfare, employment, contract rights, accreditation, fraud, or legal exposure, seek the underlying record or route the issue to the proper professional authority.

The transformation should become more factual as it proceeds, not more political.
